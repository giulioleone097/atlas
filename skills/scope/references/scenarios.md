# Explore approaches and review the decision

Conditional procedure owned by scope's decision engine (`atlasme.md`). Run for a material open choice or an explicit brainstorming/scenario request; return to the caller without implementing, publishing or creating another plan.

1. Establish one shared brief: observable outcome/proof, sourced facts, actual constraints, decision horizon and consequential unknowns. Separate user values from engineering choices. Reuse prior reviewed decisions while their inputs still hold; changing the entry skill is not new evidence.

2. Develop two or three materially different feasible approaches, including keeping or simplifying the current approach when viable. Explain an infeasible alternative's violated constraint once. Do not pad a mechanical or already settled choice with artificial alternatives. Apply the minimum-effective ladder to each approach before comparing it.

3. Separate what we control (the approaches) from what may happen (the scenarios). Use the same relevant baseline, adverse and plausible favorable conditions across approaches; omit conditions that cannot change the choice. Trace actor or subsystem incentives, first reaction, downstream response and resulting constraint when these interactions matter. Ground those actors and conditions in sources; label inferred reactions and forecasts as hypotheses. A narrated simulation is not a measurement, calibrated probability or proof of future success.

4. Compare the branches against the original outcome: expected effect, time/effort or operating cost with its source or `unknown`, dependencies, technical reach, reversibility, opportunity cost and concrete failure trigger. Preserve signal versus noise: investigate only unknowns that could change the decision. No invented scores, probability percentages or consensus-as-evidence. Use one compact table; a diagram is optional when interactions are clearer visually.

5. Before recommending a material choice, obtain a read-only decision review from the existing `atlas-reviewer` (`atlas_reviewer` on Codex) when available. Supply `mode: decision`, the shared brief, alternatives/branches, sources, constraints and one lens; ask it to challenge the case from the evidence before reading the lead's preference. This is a proposal review, not the later implementation review. No diff or fabricated artifact is required.

6. One reviewer usually suffices. Use two or three independent reviewers only when interacting domains, consequential tradeoffs or conflicting evidence justify distinct lenses such as feasibility, adverse consequences and time/cost. Give them the same factual seed and disjoint questions; gather independent findings before reconciliation. The lead verifies claims and resolves disagreement by constraints and evidence, not a vote. Retain material dissent and the observation that would change the recommendation. If native delegation is unavailable, perform a labelled self-review using those lenses; never present role-play as independent agents.

7. Choose the best-supported feasible approach under known goals and values, stating the decisive tradeoff and sensitivity to assumptions. A user's value/goal choice follows `asking.md`; an already authorized engineering choice needs no second approval. A missing load-bearing fact yields the smallest evidence checkpoint: bounded action, required inputs, observation, stop condition and which branch its result would favor. Return it unexecuted; the caller owns permission and execution. Material unaddressed objections keep the decision conditional or blocked.

8. Return the compact decision evidence into the caller's existing tree/map or owner note: approach, scenario consequences, selected/rejected alternatives, source/revision, review (`independent` with actual agent identity/lens or `self-review`), dissent, next checkpoint and reopening trigger. Do not create a separate council transcript, backlog or duplicate decision document. Review only changes that invalidate the verdict; stop unchanged debate when the next missing observation is clear.

Stop after returning a reviewed choice, a required user decision or an evidence checkpoint. The caller continues authorized work and checks actual results against this forecast.
