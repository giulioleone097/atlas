# Explore the degree of change

Conditional input to `scenarios.md`, not a separate brainstorming, review or implementation loop. Use for `--evolve [next|leap|transform]` or a request to evolve, make a major leap or rethink the approach. Bare `--evolve` compares the feasible levels; an explicit level focuses exploration without committing to that level.

| Level | Question that distinguishes its approach |
|---|---|
| next | Which small change improves the observed outcome within the current model? |
| leap | Which bottleneck, dependency or boundary could be removed or redesigned for a substantially different result? |
| transform | Which assumption about the solution, architecture or delivery model could change while preserving the user's actual outcome and hard constraints? |

Read the caller's context receipt, baseline observation, past attempts and actual constraints first. A metaphor such as quantum leap means a potentially transformative approach, never a quantified gain or scientific prediction. Do not invent targets, a tenfold benefit, resource budgets or a need for a larger system.

Generate only materially different viable approaches at the requested levels. Include keeping/removing/simplifying when useful, and mark a level infeasible or evidence-missing rather than fabricating an ambitious alternative. Separate a hard user requirement from a changeable implementation habit. A changed user goal, risk tolerance or external commitment requires that user's decision; a technical simplification inside current authority does not.

For each approach return: level, actual constraint/assumption changed, proposed mechanism, expected benefit labelled hypothesis, sourced time/cost or unknown, dependencies, reversibility and migration risk, cross-domain consequences, and the cheapest observation that could falsify its case. Do not merge controllable ambition levels with uncertain future conditions: `scenarios.md` applies the same relevant conditions to these approaches.

The existing decision reviewer challenges the mechanism, feasibility, hidden costs and whether the evidence supports the claimed degree of change. It may favor a small step or an experiment; ambition and agent agreement are not evidence. The lead selects using the original outcome and constraints, retaining material dissent and the observation that would change the choice.

Return these approach candidates to the existing scenario comparison. The caller's howto map owns selection, authorized checkpoint/implementation, actual proof and resumption. Reuse its stable ids and issue links; do not create an evolution backlog, extra approval gate, universal council or another plan.
